## Significant discoveries

**One — The Rule 6 user-led empirical battery surfaced one operator-UX gap (empty `%SYS.Task.Description`) that no automated test would have caught.** The catch surface here is identical to Epic 5/6's "user-led manual-test catches operator-rendered surface bugs" pattern — the empty Description is operator-visible in Mgmt Portal Task Manager, but not exercised by any unit-test or integration-test code path. Continues to support Epic 6 retro AI-4 observation (bug-class catch-surface consistency: certain bug shapes are only visible to a human looking at the running system).

**Two — The Story 7.1 design-decision-then-Story-7.2-implementation handoff worked exactly as Rule 9 intends.** Story 7.1's AC-5 explicitly named Story 7.2 as the index-decision carrier; Story 7.2's spec then made the index addition AC-5 (Rule 8 fix-now); Story 7.3 measured the impact (0.80s vs 60s budget). This is the cleanest application of Rule 9 (binding deferrals on named successor) seen across Epics 1–7. The lead writing Story 7.2's spec didn't have to grep `deferred-work.md` because the Rule 9 binding lived inside Story 7.1's Implementation hint stanza — same pattern, slightly faster path.

**Three — The lead's Rule 6 violation (jumping to retro before empirical battery) is a recurrence of Epic 1 / Epic 2 lead-redirect failures.** The sharpened wording from Epic 2 retro (*"the lead does NOT propose the retrospective until the epic-end empirical battery transcript exists in the conversation"*) was supposed to make this self-blocking. It didn't. The user redirect (*"do a manual test of the epic scope"*) was the rule's enforcement mechanism. The lead's empirical battery, once run, surfaced finding C-1 that would have shipped silently otherwise. This is the same shape of incident that prompted the original Rule 6 sharpening; the rule is necessary but the lead's adherence is brittle. Possible response: codify Rule 6's enforcement as an explicit pre-flight checklist item at end-of-epic ("Before the lead proposes the retro, the lead MUST emit the standard battery transcript inline, not just claim the regression sweep passed"). See AI-3 below.

**Four — Sample-production state drift across cycle resumes is a recurring friction we should automate around.** Epic 4 / Epic 5 / Epic 6 / Epic 7 all surfaced "production stopped between stories" detours. Epic 4 retro AI-4 codified the Rule 7 sub-clause (Step 1 sprint-planning probe), but on Epic 7 the probe happened at per-story time, not at sprint-planning time — so the production stopped between Story 7.1 and Story 7.2 anyway. The cleanest fix: at /epic-cycle Step 1, the lead emits a one-time `Ens.Director.IsProductionRunning` check + auto-Bootstrap if needed, so the entire epic runs against a known-running production. See AI-4 below.
